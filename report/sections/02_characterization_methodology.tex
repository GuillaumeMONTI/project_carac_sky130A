\section{Characterization Methodology}
\label{sec:characterization_methodology}


% ============================================================
% Environment

\subsection{Simulation Environment}

The characterization flow uses:

\begin{itemize}
	\item Xschem for schematic capture and simulation testbenches;
	\item ngspice for DC and AC analyses;
	\item SKY130A compact device models;
	\item Python for numerical post-processing and plotting.
\end{itemize}

Unless otherwise stated, simulations use the nominal TT model at
\qty{27}{\degreeCelsius}.

\begin{itemize}
	\item a gate-voltage sweep to obtain transfer and operating-point

	\item a drain-voltage sweep to obtain output-characteristic
	families.

\subsection{Extracted Design Quantities}

The characterization is organized around quantities that can be reused
directly during transistor sizing.

\begin{table}[H]
	\centering
	\small
	\caption{Purpose of the principal extracted quantities.}
	\label{tab:quantity_purpose}
	
	\begin{tabular}{lp{0.67\textwidth}}
		\toprule
		Quantity & Design use \\
		\midrule
		
		$I_D(V_{GS})$ &
		Current capability and gate-bias selection. \\
		
		$I_D(V_{DS})$ &
		Output behaviour, saturation region and available current. \\
		
		$V_{TH}$ &
		Threshold reference and geometry dependence. \\
		
		$V_{DSAT}$ &
		Drain-source headroom required for saturation. \\
		
		$g_m/I_D$ &
		Direct transconductance-efficiency reference for analog sizing. \\
		
		$C_G$ &
		Estimate of the capacitive cost associated with MOS gates. \\
		
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabular}
\end{table}

For the drain-current extraction, the ngspice voltage-source current
convention is accounted for using

\begin{equation}
	I_D=-I(V_{DS}).
\end{equation}

Where available, $V_{TH}$ and $V_{DSAT}$ are taken directly from the
compact-model operating-point information.

\subsection{MOS Capacitance Characterization}

The standard NMOS and PMOS devices are also evaluated as two-terminal
MOS capacitors.

For the NMOS, drain, source and bulk are shorted to ground. For the
PMOS, the same terminals are connected to the
\qty{1.8}{\volt} supply.

\begin{figure}[H]
	\centering
	\includegraphics[width=0.68\textwidth]
	{figures/schematics/tb_mos_01v8_moscap.pdf}
	\caption{Combined NMOS and PMOS gate-capacitance characterization
		testbench.}
	\label{fig:tb_mos_01v8_moscap}
\end{figure}

A unit-magnitude small-signal AC excitation is applied to the gate at
\qty{1}{\mega\hertz}. The equivalent gate capacitance is obtained from

\begin{equation}
	C_G =
	\frac{\left|\operatorname{Im}(I_G)\right|}
	{2\pi f}.
\end{equation}

The retained results are $C_G$ versus capacitor voltage, used to
quantify bias dependence, and $C_G/(WL)$ versus channel length, used
to quantify geometry dependence and estimate effective capacitance
density.
